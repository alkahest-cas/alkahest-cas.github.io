Green's functions and fundamental solutions

Experimental. Everything on this page lives in alkahest.experimental (Rust: alkahest_cas::pde::green) and may change between minor releases.

A Green's function turns a linear boundary-value problem into an integral: u = ∫ G f + boundary terms. Alkahest builds them four ways:

function what it builds result
fundamental_solution the whole-space kernel of a constant-coefficient operator FundamentalSolution
image_green_function half- and quarter-spaces, strips and slabs, the disk and ball, by images GreenFunction
green_function a SturmLiouville problem: the closed form by variation of parameters, or the eigenfunction series (static, heat, wave) GreenFunction
green_solve the solution of a PDE as ∫ G f + boundary terms GreenSolution

Convention. G solves L G = δ for the operator exactly as written, which is Mathematica's GreenFunction convention: Δ G = δ gives G = log(r)/(2π) in the plane, with no minus sign. Evolution kernels are causal (L G = δ(x) δ(t), zero for t < 0), and Helmholtz kernels are outgoing (e^{+ikr}). The Sturm–Liouville Green's functions are for 𝓛u = −[(p u')' + q u], the sign convention of SturmLiouville (𝓛φₙ = λₙ w φₙ).

#How an answer is checked

verified is True only when every check is an exact proof. A numeric check can make Alkahest refuse an answer, but it never certifies one. Each result carries its checks (dicts with what, status, method and detail) and the theorem that turns them into a proof of L G = δ:

  • L G = 0 off the singular set, by substitution. Residuals with Bessel functions are reduced by the three-term recurrences.
  • For 1-D operators, the jump formula: continuity of G and a jump of 1/a₂ in ∂G at the source prove L G = δ distributionally.
  • For elliptic kernels in 2 and 3 dimensions, unit flux through a small sphere around the source. For K₁ and H₁⁽¹⁾ this uses the DLMF leading terms.
  • For the heat kernel, unit mass and the transfer function.
  • For wave kernels, ∫ G dx = t, and for Klein–Gordon and telegraph, the value 1 of the Riemann function on the characteristics.
  • For images, the wall conditions by substitution. An image series is checked term by term with the pairs that cancel (j ↔ −j, j ↔ 1 − j).
  • For variation of parameters, continuity, the jump −1/p(ξ), both end conditions, symmetry and Abel's identity.

A construction that fails its own check is withheld with E-PDE-255.

#Fundamental solutions

Python
import alkahest as ak
import alkahest.experimental as akx

p = ak.ExprPool()
x, y = p.symbol("x"), p.symbol("y")

fs = akx.fundamental_solution("laplace", [x, y])
assert fs.verified
print(fs.kernel)        # log(sqrt(x^2 + y^2)) / (2 pi)
print(fs.identity)      # Δ G = δ(x)
print(sorted({c["what"] for c in fs.checks}))

The space variables stand for x − ξ. The operators are "laplace", "biharmonic", "helmholtz" and "modified_helmholtz" (k=), "heat" (diffusivity=), "wave" (speed=), "klein_gordon" (speed=, mass=) and "telegraph" (speed=, damping=, for u_tt + 2a u_t − c² Δu). All of them are available in 1 to 3 space dimensions. The 2-D Klein–Gordon and telegraph kernels (cos, cosh of (m/c)√(c²t² − r²)) are proved by descent from the 3-D wave kernel; the 3-D ones (a light-cone δ plus a J₁ / I₁ tail) by the dimension walk E₃ = −(2πr)⁻¹ ∂ᵣ E₁ from the 1-D kernel. For Laplace and heat, dimension=n, radius=r gives the radial kernel in n dimensions with a symbolic n. A 1-D kernel is proved by the jump formula:

Python
k = p.symbol("k", "positive")
fs1 = akx.fundamental_solution("helmholtz", [x], k=k)
assert fs1.verified
print(fs1.kernel)       # −i e^{ik|x|} / (2k)
print(fs1.identity_proof)

Each record also has derivation, references, support, radial (as a function of r, and t for evolution kernels) and side_conditions.

#The method of images

region is ("half_space", axis), ("quarter_space", a, b), ("strip", axis, width), ("disk", R) or ("ball", R), where the axes index space. walls gives one "dirichlet" or "neumann" per wall. For a strip, the wall at 0 comes first. Strips and slabs come back as an infinite image Sum. The disk and ball come from Kelvin inversion, and their result includes the Poisson kernel ∂G/∂n_ξ:

Python
xi, eta = p.symbol("xi"), p.symbol("eta")
R = p.symbol("R", "positive")
g = akx.image_green_function("laplace", [x, y], [xi, eta], ("disk", R), "dirichlet")
assert g.verified
print(g.method)          # kelvin
print(g.poisson_kernel)

A Neumann wall on the disk gives the Neumann function N = (1/4π) log(|x − ξ|² Q/R²) − (|x|² + |ξ|²)/(4πR²), with ΔN = δ − 1/(πR²) and ∂N/∂n = 0 (no Green's function with ΔG = δ exists there). A Helmholtz strip or slab gives the transverse mode series with outgoing longitudinal kernels, κₙ = √(k² − λₙ) on the principal branch (the radiation condition); at a cutoff k² = λₙ it refuses with E-PDE-260:

Python
k = p.symbol("k", "positive")
L = p.symbol("L", "positive")
wg = akx.image_green_function("helmholtz", [x, y], [xi, eta], ("strip", 1, L), ["dirichlet", "dirichlet"], k=k)
assert wg.verified and wg.method == "mode_series"

When images do not apply, Alkahest refuses with E-PDE-252, for example on a Neumann ball:

Python
z, zeta = p.symbol("z"), p.symbol("zeta")
try:
    akx.image_green_function("laplace", [x, y, z], [xi, eta, zeta], ("ball", p.integer(1)), "neumann")
except Exception as e:
    print(e)             # E-PDE-252 …

#The Neumann problem on a disk

neumann_disk_solve(x, y, R, f, g, theta) solves Δu = f in the disk with ∂u/∂r = g(θ) on the circle, for a polynomial f and a trigonometric polynomial g. A solution exists only when ∫ f dA = ∮ g ds; this compatibility condition is decided exactly, and E-PDE-257 reports that it fails. The solution is unique up to an additive constant, which is returned as constant:

Python
th = p.symbol("theta")
s = akx.neumann_disk_solve(x, y, R, 0, ak.cos(th), th)
assert s.verified
print(s.value)           # C + x
try:
    akx.neumann_disk_solve(x, y, R, 0, p.integer(1), th)
except Exception as e:
    print(e)             # E-PDE-257 …

#Counting eigenvalues below the threshold

count_lower_eigenvalues(SturmLiouville(...), params) gives a certified count of the eigenvalues below λ₀ = −q/w (the negative ones when q = 0) for constant coefficients and Robin ends. The count comes from the positive roots of G(κ) = A cosh κL + (Bκ² + C) sinh(κL)/κ, using outward-rounded interval arithmetic, a proved tail bound and monotonicity from G'. When it cannot separate the roots it refuses with E-PDE-259; it never guesses:

Python
two = akx.SturmLiouville(x, (0, 1), (("robin", 3, 1), ("robin", -3, 1)))
n = akx.count_lower_eigenvalues(two)
print(n.count, n.enclosures)   # 2 [(0, …), (1, …)]

#Two-point problems and eigenfunction series

green_function takes a SturmLiouville problem (see sturm_liouville). With kind="closed" it builds the closed form by variation of parameters. With "static", "heat" or "wave" it builds the eigenfunction series from the problem's Eigensystem. Transcendental eigenvalues stay as RootOfFamily. When the family is in closed form, the series is proved to be the expansion of the closed form: ∫ G φₙ w dx = φₙ(ξ)/λₙ for symbolic n.

Python
L, h = p.symbol("L", "positive"), p.symbol("h", "positive")
sl = akx.SturmLiouville(x, (0, L), ("dirichlet", ("robin", h, 1)))
closed = akx.green_function(sl, xi, "closed")
series = akx.green_function(sl, xi, "static")
assert closed.verified and series.verified
print(closed.branches)   # (G for x < ξ, G for x > ξ)

If λ = 0 is an eigenvalue, no Green's function exists and "closed" refuses with E-PDE-254. In that case the static series is the modified Green's function, which is orthogonal to the zero mode:

Python
nn = akx.SturmLiouville(x, (0, L), ("neumann", "neumann"))
try:
    akx.green_function(nn, xi, "closed")
except Exception as e:
    print(e)             # E-PDE-254 …
g = akx.green_function(nn, xi, "static")
print(g.method)          # modified_eigenfunction_series

#Solving with a Green's function

green_solve(pde) returns u = ∫ G f + boundary terms for three classes of problem:

  • two-point problems a₂u'' + a₁u' + a₀u = F with Robin data at both ends, reduced to Sturm–Liouville form by an integrating factor;
  • the heat and wave equations on the line with sources, by Duhamel's principle;
  • Poisson's equation in the upper half-plane, by the Poisson kernel.

The integrals are closed wherever integrate closes them, and the result is verified by substitution. A GreenSolution has the shape of pdsolve's PdeSolution: kind ("closed_form" or "integral_representation"), value, closed_part, terms, the green function it used, checks and steps.

Python
U = akx.Function("u")
u = U(x)
pde = (
    akx.PDE(-akx.D(u, x, 2) - p.integer(1), u)
    .on(akx.Interval(x, p.integer(0), p.integer(1)))
    .bc(ak.subs(u, {x: p.integer(0)}), 0)
    .bc(ak.subs(u, {x: p.integer(1)}), 0)
)
sol = akx.green_solve(pde)
assert sol.verified
print(sol.kind, sol.value)

A problem outside these classes is refused with E-PDE-256.

#Klein–Gordon and the telegraph equation

transform_solve returns Klein–Gordon and telegraph problems on the line as light-cone integrals with Bessel kernels, not as Fourier integrals:

u = e^{−Dt/2}(f(x − ct) + f(x + ct))/2 + ∫_{x−ct}^{x+ct} K_f f + ∫ K_g g.

For Klein–Gordon the kernels are built from J₀((m/c)√(c²t² − x²)), and for the telegraph equation from e^{−at} I₀(…). Each kernel is verified to solve the equation, and its Riemann function is verified to equal 1 on the characteristics.

Python
t = p.symbol("t", "positive")
v = U(x, t)
kg = (
    akx.PDE(akx.D(v, t, 2) - akx.D(v, x, 2) + v, v)
    .on(akx.Line(x) * akx.HalfLine(t, 0))
    .ic(ak.subs(v, {t: p.integer(0)}), ak.exp(-(x**2)))
    .ic(ak.subs(akx.D(v, t), {t: p.integer(0)}), 0)
)
sol = akx.transform_solve(kg)
assert sol.verified
print(sol.kind)          # integral_representation
print(sol.terms[0]["kernel"])

#Comparison with Mathematica

The corpus tests/pde_corpus/green_problems.json has 59 problems from Strauss (ch. 3 and 7), Evans (§2.2–2.4), Haberman (ch. 9 and 11) and Duffy. tests/pde_corpus/mathematica/green_ref.txt holds the Mathematica 14.3 references (GreenFunction / DSolveValue, 120 s per problem), and tests/pde_corpus/score_m5.py prints the table.

category problems Alkahest, exactly verified Mathematica, correct closed form
fundamental solutions 20 20 9 (+1 wrong)
symbolic dimension n 2 2 0
images 14 14 0
two-point closed forms 7 7 (one is the expected E-PDE-254) 6
eigenfunction series 6 6 2 (2 not queried)
green_solve 10 10 9 (+1 left as an integral)
total 59 59 26

Where both systems return a closed form, the values agree at the sample points. The one disagreement is the 3-D heat kernel. Mathematica returns e^{−r²/(4κt)} / (4πκ t^{3/2}), which is missing a factor (4π)^{−1/2}, so its mass is √(4π) rather than 1. tests/test_pde_m5.py documents this.

#Errors

E-PDE-250 (no kernel here: a symbolic dimension for an operator other than Laplace and heat), 251 (malformed input), 252 (images do not apply), 253 (the series cannot be assembled), 254 (no Green's function exists: λ = 0 is an eigenvalue), 255 (a construction refuted by its own check, withheld), 256 (green_solve does not recognise the problem), 257 (the Neumann compatibility condition fails), 258 (Neumann data outside the closed-form class), 259 (an eigenvalue count that cannot be certified), 260 (a waveguide at a cutoff). See Error handling.
